# Generations: age, sex, relationships, births, and names

Status: active (started 2026-10-10). Follows [`LIVING_WORLD.md`](LIVING_WORLD.md).

## Goal

Make the band a population that renews itself, so language, lore, and relationships can be passed
down and drift over generations. Like the rest of the project, the rules are physical and social
constraints; who pairs with whom, who does what, where couples live, and what people are called
should emerge.

Time scale: **one simulated hour is one year of life.** Adulthood comes at about 15 hours, so
several generations fit in a headless run of a few minutes.

## Steps

### G1. Fixes that matter more with a growing population

- Deer herds recover in proportion to what's left, so hunting doesn't empty the valley.
- People who know wolves are dangerous prefer company while wolves are about.

### G2. Age and sex

- Every person has a sex and a birth time. Founders start at varied adult ages.
- Life stages: carried baby (to ~3), child (to ~15), adult, elder (from ~50).
- Realistic constraints, no built-in roles: women can conceive from ~15 to ~45, men stay fertile
  longer; men are somewhat stronger on average (a modest edge in strikes), with a lot of overlap.
- Gradual frailty from ~50: slower, tire faster, heal less; death from old age becomes likelier
  each year, so lifespans vary.

### G3. Relationships

- Known kin: parents, children, and siblings are recognized; help and learning go to kin first.
- Trust can go negative: someone whose tips led nowhere, who refused help, or took your food
  becomes someone you avoid and don't believe.
- Reciprocity: people remember favours and are more likely to return them.
- Grief: when someone close dies, those left behind stay near home and seek company for a while.

### G4. Couples and births

- Pair bonds form between adults who have spent a lot of time together and trust each other; they
  fade if the pair drifts apart. Partners stay near each other and share food.
- People raised near each other as children aren't attracted to each other (the Westermarck
  effect), which covers siblings and pushes pairing across camps. The aversion fades for someone
  who has known no other possible partner for a long time.
- Conception depends on conditions: a well-fed, rested woman with a partner, a few years after her
  last child. Late pregnancy slows her down. Babies are carried and nursed (the mother eats more),
  then toddle after a parent and learn by watching, as children do today.
- Children's personalities blend their parents' with variation.
- Where couples live is not a rule.
- If the band dwindles, newcomers wander in.

### G5. Names

- After a birth, the mother picks a sound she doesn't use for anything and says it while holding
  or pointing at the baby; whoever watches learns it like any word. Founders know each other's
  names from the start.
- Names are stored with acquaintances (bounded), not in the word list.
- Names are used when calling someone (requests, corrections) and can be misheard or differ by
  family.

### G6. Generations

- A long-run study mode and a measure of how people's words change between generations.
- Viewer: shape for sex, smaller children, grey-edged elders, carried babies, partner lines,
  names, and feed lines for births, couples, grief, and deaths of old age.

## Measures

Population over time (births, deaths by cause and age, people standing at the end), pairs formed
within and across families, how words and lore pass from parents to children, and the success
test (episodes across 40 valleys).
